    Step 0 status and findings¶

    Where the prototypes and mock-ups of Step 0 stand, and what they found. Each prototype's README in prototypes/ holds its full numbers and its "Not covered" list; this page is the summary. Updated 2026-11-25; see "Resume here" for the handover.

    Findings¶

    Strategies. On three scenes and three machines GLOMAP maps 3–4 times faster than COLMAP's incremental mapper with the same pose accuracy (rotation within a few tenths of a degree, position under a centimetre apart). GLOMAP's reprojection error is about twice COLMAP's on every scene and machine (1.9–2.9 px against 1.1–1.3 px); the cause is not known.

    Scoring. A camera a mapper poses without any 3D point is not part of the model. GLOMAP posed one such photo on kicker about 47 m from its true place, and including it in the alignment turned a 0.6° result into 13°. Every score now counts only cameras that hold points.

    Machines and builds. - pycolmap 4.2.1 from PyPI has no CUDA and no Linux ARM wheel. On the GB10 the conda-forge build (CUDA) is used through micromamba. It runs SIFT extraction and matching on the GPU, about three times faster than the CPU on the X3 or the M4 Pro. - Runs on different machines give the same quality but not identical models: GLOMAP joined kicker's problem photo on the M4 Pro and the GB10, not on the X3. - Registration of a 38-image scene peaks at 28 GB resident memory (courtyard, M4 Pro). - Poisson meshing in the conda-forge pycolmap build for the GB10 aborts ("Failed to close loop"), even on a synthetic sphere. Delaunay meshing works and is used. - COLMAP upstream has a HIP backend since 2026-10 (PR #4795). Built from main in a ROCm 7.2.4 container for gfx1151, it runs dense depth on the X3 at about 2.7 times the GB10's time, at 98% GPU use; one run aborted with a memory-aperture violation and a repeat passed (not root-caused). Nothing is installed on the host.

    GB10 GPU time. The DGX serves production LLMs in gpu-mode prod, which leaves about 15 GB of memory. Recce GPU work runs in a free window: by day under the owner's standing go, switched by the Recce session as markodragoljevic, switched back to prod and verified by the homelab session. Dense depth and a splat together used up to 50 GB.

    Quality state. Coverage per region is enough for an agent to find a removed region, when the removal is local: choosing the region whose removal touches the fewest neighbours matters, since a naive choice left 12 of 22 regions at zero coverage and the test unanswerable.

    RAW. A minimal pipeline (rawpy, one white balance and one exposure for the whole set) registers as well as the camera JPEGs, with the most even colour. Chemigram, as driven here, registered 7 of 14 photos with dark, yellow-green renders; stock darktable on the same RAW files is neutral, so the cause lies in Chemigram's presets or the recipe used. Not investigated.

    Robot capture sets. With exact poses the model lands within 0.021% scale and 0.18% placement; the frame conversion (OpenGL camera axes, z-up world, {x, y, z, w} quaternions) is checked by render, and both wrong-convention controls fail.

    Poses decide dense quality. On courtyard the same dense stage scores F1 0.35 at 5 cm from RC-P1's poses and 0.88 from ETH3D's ground-truth poses; RC-P1's cameras sit a median 6.1 cm from the truth after alignment. Pose refinement before dense is the first lever, ahead of any dense setting. Why RC-P1's poses are that far off is not investigated.

    Splat quality. gsplat at 30,000 steps reaches PSNR 27.7 on Mip-NeRF 360 garden and 29.2 on counter, in line with published gsplat figures. On courtyard (7,000 steps) it stays low: 18.4 dB from RC-P1's poses, 20.9 from ground-truth poses, so poses explain part of it; 30,000 steps from RC-P1's poses give 18.2 dB, so the step count does not. Garden used 14.3 GiB of GPU memory, close to what prod mode leaves free.

    Texture. texrecon (BSD; dependencies BSD and MPL-2.0) textures the courtyard mesh in 52 s on the GB10; 16% of the faces are seen by no photo.

    Learned poses. MapAnything's Apache checkpoint alone is 0.9–1.7° and 12–37 cm off on ETH3D; bundle-adjusted from the same SIFT matches it comes close to COLMAP (0.36–0.82° relative against 0.40–0.69°). The adjustment lets the scale drift (−41% and −74% on two ETH3D runs), so the adjusted model is anchored to MapAnything's camera centres. On ARKitScenes it fails on one low-texture video (82° relative) and is wrong in parts on a textured one; COLMAP registers 10–18% of the frames of the low-texture videos. MapAnything needs 42–59 GiB of GPU memory for 90–218 views, more than prod mode leaves. The agreement check (learned against each COLMAP component, within 2° relative rotation and 2% of extent, no ground truth) passes all six ETH3D runs and rejects every broken learned model; its one miss is a COLMAP component that is itself 13.8° off. It can confirm the learned model only where COLMAP also registered, and on these runs the frames only the learned model has are where it fails.

    Underwater. Refraction is a scale error, not noise: on AquaMVS a pinhole calibration is off by −0.03% to −4.8% depending on where the board is, and a refractive model by at most ±0.28%. Colour underwater needs the RAW: SQUID's published TIFs clip red to zero on 78–99.9% of pixels. Over 6–19 m on the Satil wreck the colour cast is nearly one global tint, which a single white balance removes; what changes with range is contrast (0.39 to 0.07 on the grey ramp), and the simple per-image backscatter fit restores it unevenly, whichever model is fitted (simple or Sea-thru's form): near the camera the deck holds no dark objects, so the dark-pixel estimate overshoots. Our own pipeline gets no depth from SQUID's few hazy stereo pairs without the rig calibration. SQUID's chart annotations sit a few pixels off its published undistorted images and are snapped before use.

    Decided by the owner (2026-11-27)

    • The cave tolerance is 2% or 5 cm; on it the cave segment is not met (11 of 17).
    • RC-P10 stays not proven with its cause recorded; pose refinement moves to the build phase.
    • RC-P13 closes as partly proven, each segment's verdict recorded.
    • Step 0 ends when the mock-ups (M2, M3, M4, M6, M7) have the owner's verdicts and a close-out report is written for RC-01 Vision. The report is written: Step 0 close-out report (2026-11-27).
    • AI is optional; Recce is complete without it. Every feature works from geometry and rules alone and is good enough on its own. AI is opt-in: Recce is an MCP server for a person's own agent, and a person can bring their own AI (an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, hosted or local) to take features further. Recorded in the brief; the mock-ups show both states.
    • The first case: the owner's home, captured with the iPhone app (owner, 2026-11-27). The build starts with the iPhone capture app (RC-P11) and the owner photographing at home, from simpler to more complex: objects first, then a whole room. The room is the setting the robots map, so it leads into the robot use cases. Recorded in the brief.
    • Learn mode is a feature inside the product (owner, 2026-11-27): the mini-labs and their tutor (RC-M5) live in Recce beside what they explain, not as a separate face.
    • Distribution is at the front, for work in the field (owner, 2026-11-27). The owner travels with a laptop and shoots with a camera, a drone or an underwater camera, and processes the day's work after. The answer is not a stronger laptop but offloading: the laptop sends work to the owner's other machines and to rented GPUs. Running workloads on other machines, rented GPUs included, is built from the beginning and is a key part of the vision, not a later scaling step. Open problems this raises: the upload (1,500 photos at 50 MB are 75 GB), whether the RAW files need to leave the laptop at all, and how work splits between the laptop and the remote workers before anything is sent. Recorded in the brief.
    • Both: Recce Studio on the desktop, and the web (owner, 2026-11-27): the web application packaged with the local engine in Electron, for macOS and Windows; RAW processing stays local.

    Standing rules that carry over

    • Approved downloads and installs (user-level only): ETH3D and Mip-NeRF 360 data, MapAnything (Apache weights only), PyTorch, gsplat, texrecon, micromamba, Open3D, ARKitScenes subset, AquaMVS, SQUID, Blender, rawpy, the MCP SDK (RC-P3); three.js and Spark from a CDN and rerun-sdk (RC-P6's environment); scipy, tifffile and opencv-python-headless (RC-P12's environment). In throwaway Docker containers only (nothing on the host): CloudCompare, RTKLIB and laspy (2026-11-26). Datasets added 2026-11-26: the Etna lava tube (CC BY 4.0), two more ARKitScenes Faro visits, EuroSDR RPAS Phase 2/3 (CC BY 4.0) and UseGeo Dataset-1 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; fetched with certificate checks off by the owner's decision, the host's certificate having expired in 2024). Anything else is asked first.
    • Recipes and profiles stay private (owner, 2026-11-26). What evaluation teaches (per-device corrections such as a focal-length fix, calibrated uncertainty models, tuned settings per capture scenario, the evaluation corpus and its scores) will live as data files in a separate private repository, next to the owner's data; the public engine loads them from a path and falls back to public defaults. Not built yet; new work keeps engine code generic and tuned values separable, so they can move there.
    • DGX GPU windows: standing go by day. Message the homelab session before switching; switch with ssh markodragoljevic@dgx-llm-1 '~/bin/gpu-mode-swap.sh free --json'; when the work's processes have exited, send it "done"; it switches back to prod and verifies both vLLMs. The claude account (reached through the Mac mini) runs the jobs and cannot switch modes.
    • The X3 is shared with eval-harness, which announces its test runs; heavy Recce work stays sequential and off cores it pins. The HIP build is the Docker image recce-p2-colmap-hip:latest.
    • Gate: make check; echo EXIT=$? before every commit, never a filtered line; commits land on main and CI is watched to a verdict.
    • Two parallel streams at most, on sonnet subagents; each writes only its own prototype folder and never commits.
