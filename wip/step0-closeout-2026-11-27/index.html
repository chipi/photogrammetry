    Step 0 close-out report (2026-11-27)¶

    The input to RC-01 Vision. It states what Step 0 set out to prove, what it proved and did not, what the mock-ups settled, and what carries into the build. The numbers come from Step 0 status and findings and from each prototype's and mock-up's README, which hold the full results and their "Not covered" lists; this report adds no new measurement.

    What Step 0 set out to do¶

    Two tracks ran side by side (brief §5). The prototypes (RC-P1 to RC-P13) prove how the technology works; a prototype counts as proven when it meets its stated criterion, and a failure with its evidence is also a result. The mock-ups (RC-M1 to RC-M8) work out how the product looks and behaves; a mock-up is done when the owner has judged its concepts on screen. Step 0 ends when the mock-ups have the owner's verdicts and this report is written (owner, 2026-11-27). Everything ran on public datasets with published references; the owner's own captures and the friend's dive set come after.

    What the evidence says about the vision¶

    The vision sentence under consideration in the brief is: "A reconstruction engine that always says what it knows and what it does not — scale, frame, coverage, uncertainty — so that a person, a robot or an agent can decide what to capture next, above water and below." Step 0 tested each part of it.

    Scale comes from priors and references, never from pixels alone. With exact poses a robot's set lands within 0.021% scale (RC-P9); RTK priors on half a drone's photos fix scale to +0.025% (RC-P8, RC-P13); a learned model's metric scale is off by up to 41% (RC-P8), so it never sets scale. A photos-only reconstruction has no scale, and the mock-ups say so on screen (RC-M1's "scale: arbitrary"). The drone and building segments show a second limit: a focal length 0.6–1.3% long trades against flying height, so the surface reads 0.3–1.1 m low until control points or a calibrated lens pin it.

    Frame is a contract that can be checked. The robot set's axes, handedness and quaternion order are verified by render, and both wrong conventions fail (RC-P9).

    Coverage predicts error, and an agent can read it. On RC-P10's room, surfaces no frame saw are 52.4 mm from the Faro scan and those seen by 32 or more frames 14.7 mm (RC-M2). An agent with read tools finds a held-out region (RC-P3). Where to stand next is the engine's job: an agent inventing positions put 0 of 3 shots where a photographer can stand, and 3 of 3 with the engine's spots (RC-M3).

    Uncertainty can be carried per element. A structured model tags every wall, floor and passage as measured, inferred or unknown with its sigma (RC-P13); the room meets its reference on every measured dimension and marks 1.92 m² of floor it never saw as unknown, not guessed.

    Underwater is a scale problem first. A pinhole calibration through a flat port misses scale by up to 4.8% depending on where the object is; a refractive model holds ±0.28% (RC-P12). Colour needs the RAW: the published TIFs clip red to zero on 78–99.9% of pixels. The cast at 6–19 m is one global tint; what changes with range is contrast, which the simple backscatter fit restores unevenly.

    The central bet is not yet tested. That splitting the work across machines and a rented GPU beats the best single machine (RC-P7) is the reason the project exists, and Step 0 did not run it. What exists is the ground under it: the same registration on three machines and three architectures (RC-P1), dense depth on CUDA and HIP (RC-P2), and the measurement that dense depth and a splat together need up to 50 GB of GPU memory.

    Noisy or melted geometry Courtyard F1 at 5 cm: 0.35 from our poses, 0.88 from ground-truth poses; poses decide dense quality
    Holes 16% of the courtyard mesh's faces are seen by no photo

    What the mock-ups settled¶

    Seven mock-ups were built on real prototype output, except RC-M5, which is synthetic by design. The owner kept every concept of RC-M1 to RC-M7, the coach on RC-M1 and RC-M2 included; what goes into the product is decided when the build starts. The patterns they share (visual ideas):

    • The reconstruction as a story with evidence (RC-M1, RC-M3): the run replayed camera by camera, a debrief whose every claim opens its evidence in the scene, and a claim without evidence flagged.
    • Quality on the surface (RC-M2): light and shadow by how many frames saw a surface, confidence as solid or hatched, the reference beside the estimate where one exists.
    • The gap list as the brief for the next capture (RC-M1): each gap with where to stand, what to shoot and why, a site map, and the same list as JSON for a robot.
    • Recipes as node graphs, compared by the node that differs, with cost estimated before and measured after (RC-M4).
    • A presentation that stays honest (RC-M6): a path through the photos, the splat where it holds and the mesh where it does not, and an "About this model" card on every shared link.
    • Underwater made visible (RC-M7): the water's colour against each correction, and the refraction as a lens that can be switched off.
    • A camera is its photo: every scene draws a camera as its image where it was taken.
    • AI is optional, and a coach when present (owner, 2026-11-27): every view works from geometry and rules alone; with the person's own AI connected it explains why, orders a plan from the engine's own actions, says how to know it worked and what the facts cannot say, and every number it writes is checked against the fact it cites. One recorded call each on RC-M1, M2 and M7 cost US\(0.038–0.082; RC-M5's five tutor calls cost US\)0.083 together. Unsupported statements per mock-up: 0 (RC-M1, RC-M7), 1 (RC-M2), 3 of 51 (RC-M5). One wrong statement without a number got through on RC-M1; the checker cannot catch such a statement.
    • What an AI call costs depends on its context, not its prompt: the same call cost US\(1.75 with an agent's default context and US\)0.028 without it (RC-M4).

    Decided by the owner during Step 0¶

    • AI is optional; Recce is complete without it, and is an MCP server for a person's own agent (2026-11-27).
    • The learned route is an optional second route beside COLMAP, kept only after the agreement check, never the source of scale (RC-P8).
    • Recipes, per-device corrections and tuned settings live in a separate private repository; the public engine keeps them separable (2026-11-26).
    • RC-P7 moves to the build phase; RC-P10 stays not proven with its cause recorded, pose refinement moves to the build; RC-P13 closes partly proven; the cave tolerance is 2% or 5 cm (2026-11-26 and 2026-11-27).
    • The underwater benchmark capture is decided after the rest of Step 0 (2026-11-26).

    What RC-01 Vision can take from this¶

    These are proposals for the owner, drawn from the evidence above:

    • The vision sentence holds as written: each of its parts (scale, frame, coverage, uncertainty, the next capture, above water and below) has a working first result.
    • Scale is stated, never inferred: from priors, references or control points, or reported as arbitrary. A learned model may propose poses; it never sets scale.
    • The engine supplies positions, numbers and verdicts; an AI explains, orders and coaches, and is checked against the engine.
    • Distribution remains untested; where it sits in the build's order is open.

    Decided by the owner after this report (2026-11-27): the first case is the owner's home with the iPhone app, objects first and then a whole room, leading into the robot cases; learn mode is a feature inside the product. Both are recorded in the brief §4.

    Not covered¶

    • Public data only. No owner capture and no dive set; one scene per prototype in several cases (RC-P5 on pipes, RC-P9 on its fixture, RC-P12 on one site and one rig).
    • This report summarises; it does not re-run or re-check any number. The prototype READMEs are authoritative.
    • Mock-up verdicts are on concepts, not on finished design; RC-M2 to RC-M7 had one phase each.
    • No cost model for a full capture end to end: per-stage times exist for single scenes, not for a 1,500-photo set.
