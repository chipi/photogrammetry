    Review of the concept-phase start (2026-11-23)¶

    An independent advisor review (Fable 5.1, read-only) of the brief, the architecture sketch, the use cases, the research notes and the reference documents, as they stood on 2026-11-23. Condensed; the full findings are below by heading. External facts the reviewer did not check are marked (unverified).

    1. Gaps¶

    1. "The laptop alone is a complete system" is false with the default stack: no COLMAP dense or gsplat on the M4 Pro (tools overview §18).
    2. RC-P3 tests the wrong input: fanning out features and matching on 60–150 iPhone photos takes seconds, so transfer dominates. The hours on large sets are dense depth and splat training, which run on one or two machines (GB10, X3 if HIP works). The riskiest unknowns — cutting and merging a view graph across machines (sketch Q5) and the X3 HIP dense build — are untested. "Matches a single-machine run" needs a tolerance: GPU SIFT is not bit-identical across CUDA, HIP and CPU.
    3. Splats in or out of the MVP: documents disagreed.
    4. The iPhone app contradicted the non-goals and stale "later" text in four places.
    5. Prototype dependencies inverted: RC-P4 needs ARKit poses (RC-P10 or a third-party export) and MapAnything on CUDA (the GB10 build RC-P2 proves); RC-P5 depends on a Gazebo fixture robotics-lab has offered but not built.
    6. Untestable criteria: RC-P6 ("the owner agrees"), RC-P7 ("usable"), RC-P5 and RC-P9 ("a stated tolerance", none stated); RC-P8 has none. Suggested RC-P6 test: remove a region's images, the agent names the region, add them back, show the coverage metric moved.
    7. The capture-set format cannot yet carry an iPhone capture: per-sensor intrinsics (ARKit's vary per frame with focus (unverified)), PNG-only images (no DNG or HEIC), no specified conversion from ARKit's y-up camera-to-world frame.
    8. No data-volume or storage budget: 150 D850/A1 RAWs is about 8 GB before intermediates, depth maps and caches on every worker. Deletion by hash must also purge worker and rented-box caches.
    9. Four GPU platforms is an unpriced maintenance tax; the iMac Pro costs more than it contributes.
    10. The competitive gap was overstated (WebODM, Meshroom, Brush, RealityScan with AMD; splats exist in Pix4D, Polycam, KIRI, Postshot, WebODM).
    11. Decided early without evidence: own worker protocol, SvelteKit + three.js + Spark, monorepo, X3 as coordinator. Decided late: what the MVP delivers for which use case, and any time budget for one person.
    12. Privacy and licence: traces would carry home-scan file names; Refractive COLMAP's licence is unknown; "open source later" while licence is listed open; the benchmark set holds private photos.
    13. Minor: primer §8 says COLMAP dense needs CUDA (also HIP); primer §13 attributes the range-dependent attenuation finding to Sea-thru, which applies Akkaynak and Treibitz's 2018 revised model (unverified detail).

    2. Motivation and positioning¶

    1. As a Metashape or RealityScan replacement it is not worth building; for objects, bikes and rooms free tools suffice (RealityScan Windows-only unverified).
    2. What no tool does: scale, frame and uncertainty as an output contract; quality as machine-readable state with per-image verdicts and coverage regions; robot or ARKit poses as weighted priors with a refinement statement; refraction and range-based colour.
    3. Three equal pillars is a lack of focus. The hardware pool is mostly an engineering itch for small sets; its real value is "offload from the field laptop to home, burst to a rented GPU". The photographer pillar competes with free products. Robots and agents have no incumbent.
    4. Proposed centre: the scientific reconstruction model plus agent-readable quality state, with underwater as the flagship domain.
    5. MCP is substantive only if the loop closes: the engine names a weak link, an agent turns it into a capture request for a robot or a diver, the engine re-registers incrementally. Second use: agents running the benchmark across recipes.
    6. Proposed vision sentence: "A reconstruction engine that always says what it knows and what it does not (scale, frame, coverage, uncertainty) so that a person, a robot or an agent can decide what to capture next, above water and below."

    3. Ideas for more value¶

    1. Capture judge first: a CPU-only recon judge — blur, exposure and white-balance variance, overlap, lens groups, components and weak links after a fast sparse solve — as JSON and over MCP; usable at the dive site and as a front end to other tools. About 2–3 weeks.
    2. Chemigram as the develop stage: darktable headless, gray-card white balance, state propagation across a set; reverse: range maps become a Sea-thru primitive in Chemigram. Days.
    3. Open underwater benchmark with ground truth: flat vs dome port, GoPro vs DSLR, scale bars and tags; Brown vs Refractive COLMAP vs MapAnything. A research contribution; gives RC-P8 a criterion.
    4. Robotics-lab as the first real client, closing the capture-advice loop.
    5. A repeatable motorised object rig for benchmark error bars.
    6. Rerun as the engineering viewer until the product UI earns its place.
    7. 4D reef monitoring: two dives, co-registered, change detection.
    8. GoPro GPMF gravity and gyro as SfM priors.

    4. Sequencing proposed by the reviewer¶

    This week: the shoes with the iPhone and the D850 beside an AprilTag board, COLMAP and GLOMAP on the M4 Pro and the X3; in parallel the COLMAP HIP dense build on the X3 and pycolmap + gsplat on the GB10; quality.json v0 with three MCP read tools; capture-set amendments to robotics-lab.

    Restructuring: cut the iMac Pro; merge P2 into P1; move P3 after a large set exists, with dense depth and SfM partition as the unit of work and a stated tolerance; defer P10 in favour of a Record3D or Polycam export; replace P5's Gazebo fixture with a Blender render; Chemigram in P7b; give P8 a criterion and move it ahead of P7.

    Questions for the owner before the vision¶

    1. Which single result in three months makes the project worth it?
    2. When is the next dive, with scale bars and both port types?
    3. Has the shoes set been tried in RealityScan or Apple Object Capture?
    4. Is the hardware pillar about using every machine, or about offloading from the field and bursting to rented GPUs?
    5. Storage budget and retention rule for home scans?
    6. Will robotics-lab deliver a real capture set in the first quarter, or does this project make its own fixture?
    7. Metric tolerances for RC-P5 and RC-P9, in millimetres and as a percentage.
    8. Licence: decide permissive now, or keep it open?
    9. Is the iOS app worth the weeks it displaces?
    10. Does the GB10's shared-GPU schedule leave enough CUDA time, or is a rented card the real CUDA worker?
