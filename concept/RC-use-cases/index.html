    First use cases — a thought experiment¶

    Working document. The owner's first real scans, walked through the architecture sketch one by one, as a conceptual proof: does the shape hold, what breaks, which options and recipes each case needs. Input to the prototypes, to RC-02 and to RC-04.

    Version: 0.1 · Draft · 2026-11-23

    Device facts marked (to verify) come from general knowledge and were not checked against a source this session; the prototypes check them on the real devices.

    1. The cases¶

    The owner works through the same subjects with each device, in this order:

    # Subject iPhone 15 Pro GoPro Nikon D850 Sony A1
    A Object — a Lego toy, a camera, a book, a pair of shoes UC-A1 UC-A2 UC-A3 UC-A4
    B Room — the owner's room, from inside UC-B1 UC-B2 UC-B3 UC-B4
    C Car — in a car park, outdoors UC-C1 (any device)
    D Motorbikes (two) UC-D1 (any device)
    E Mountain bike UC-E1 (any device)

    Cases A and B are the systematic series: one subject, four devices, so every device is compared on the same ground. C, D and E come along the way, with whichever device is at hand, and push the engine to its limits.

    2. The same journey, step by step¶

    Every case follows the same eight steps through the architecture. UC-A1 walks them in full; the others describe only what differs.

    1. Capture — what the device records, and what the owner should do.
    2. Into the system — which face, which files, what the ingest stage reads.
    3. Recipe — what the engine proposes from the evidence, and why.
    4. Registration — how the photos line up, and what is likely to resist.
    5. Scale and frame — where metric scale comes from, or why it is arbitrary.
    6. Products — dense cloud, mesh, texture, splat.
    7. Export — what leaves, in which format, for which tool.
    8. Quality and advice — what the engine reports, and what it tells the owner to reshoot.

    3. Case A — an object¶

    UC-A1 · Object with the iPhone 15 Pro (the full walk-through)¶

    The four objects are four different problems:

    Object What it tests Expected difficulty
    Pair of shoes Matte, textured, closed shape Easy — the clean baseline
    Book Near-planar faces, sharp edges, thin pages; a flat cover is close to the degenerate planar case for two-view geometry Medium — needs views around the edges, not only of the covers
    Lego toy Small, glossy plastic, fine detail, repeated studs (repetitive texture fools matching) Medium to hard
    Camera Black body (little texture), glass lens (transparent and reflective), metal Hard — expect holes at the lens; the splat will look better than the mesh

    1. Capture. What the iPhone offers, and what of it reaches the engine:

    Source What it gives How the engine gets it
    Main camera, 48 MP sensor; the Camera app saves 24 MP HEIF by default, 48 MP only in HEIF Max, JPEG Max or RAW Max at 1× (verified 2026-11-24) HEIC or JPEG, or ProRAW DNG Web upload, or AirDrop to the laptop then upload
    EXIF Focal length, lens id, exposure, GPS Read at ingest
    Lens switching The Camera app switches to the ultra-wide for close subjects (macro); it can be turned off with the Auto Macro button or Settings > Camera > Macro Control (verified 2026-11-24) EXIF lens id per image — the engine groups images by lens and calibrates each separately
    LiDAR depth Low-resolution metric depth Not in plain Camera photos. Only through a capture app (Record3D, Polycam, Scaniverse) or our own capture app (RC-P11)
    ARKit camera poses Metric camera positions, gravity Same: only through a capture app's export
    Portrait-mode depth A depth map embedded in the HEIC (to verify) Possible at ingest, but portrait mode is not a capture mode for photogrammetry

    The owner shoots 60–150 photos in two or three rings around the object at different heights, with overlap between neighbours, exposure and focus locked (long-press AE/AF lock), on a plain matte background, in diffuse light. The finding: the plain Camera app gives photos and EXIF only. The LiDAR and ARKit data the owner wants to reuse needs a capture app's export — which is exactly what the later iPhone app exists for. Until then there are two ways in: photos-only (Camera app) and pose-and-depth (a capture app's export).

    2. Into the system. The owner opens the web UI on the phone and uploads. Risk: Safari may convert HEIC on upload: it transcodes a file whose type is not in the page's accept list (verified 2026-11-24), so the upload page lists HEIC and DNG explicitly; what the photo picker hands over is tested on the phone — acceptable for a first test, but the ingest stage records which happened. ProRAW DNG files come through the Files picker. A capture-app export comes in as a folder: images, intrinsics, poses, depth.

    3. Recipe. Evidence: one device, 60–150 images, not sequential, no GPS worth trusting indoors, the owner tagged "object". The engine proposes object-walkaround: retrieval-plus- exhaustive pair selection (small set, so exhaustive is affordable), incremental COLMAP or GLOMAP (RC-P1 decides the default), automatic background mask. With a capture-app export, it proposes object-walkaround+priors: ARKit poses as priors, LiDAR depth as a dense-stage prior.

    4. Registration. Shoes register cleanly. The book's covers alone would be near-degenerate; views of the spine and edges carry it. Lego studs produce repetitive matches; geometric verification rejects most of the false ones, and masks keep the background from dominating. The camera's glass returns false matches that move with the viewpoint; bundle adjustment marks those points as high-error, and they are filtered. What resists: the iPhone's per-photo processing (Smart HDR, Deep Fusion) tone-maps each photo differently, and ProRAW is captured together with that processing, so it is not radiometrically linear (verified 2026-11-24). Matching tolerates it; texturing does not — hence ProRAW and the develop stage.

    5. Scale and frame. Photos-only: arbitrary scale, stated as such, unless the owner puts a marker (an AprilTag sheet) or a ruler in the scene, or enters one measured distance in the UI. With ARKit priors: metric from priors, in ARKit's gravity-aligned frame. The model's "up" is known from gravity (ARKit) or from the ground plane.

    6. Products. Dense depth on the GB10 (CUDA) or the X3 (a HIP build, unproven); Poisson mesh; texture with exposure-matched images; a splat from the same poses (on the GB10 with gsplat, or with Brush on the laptop alone).

    7. Export. GLB with textures (Blender, Godot, Unity, Unreal), USDZ (iPhone Quick Look), OBJ and PLY; the splat as GLB with KHR_gaussian_splatting and as PLY. Every export states units and up axis.

    8. Quality and advice. Registered 142/150, with the reasons for the eight rejects (blur, too little overlap). Reprojection error per image. Coverage regions: "the underside of the shoe is not covered — turn it over and shoot one more ring, keeping the background"; "the lens of the camera is missing in the mesh; it is present in the splat."

    Verdict. Works for shoes and the book; works with masks and care for Lego; the camera works as a splat and partially as a mesh. Options this case needs: lens grouping by EXIF; automatic background masks; a two-sided capture mode (object turned over between rings, merged as one model); a scale-entry tool; ProRAW through the develop stage.

    UC-A2 · Object with the GoPro¶

    Differs from A1: - Lens. GoPro photos in Wide/HyperView are strongly distorted; HERO13 photos offer Wide (16–34 mm) and Linear (19–39 mm) only, and RAW (.gpr) requires Wide (verified 2026-11-24); Linear mode is dewarped in-camera to a near-pinhole image at some cost in field of view. The recipe differs: a fisheye camera model for Wide, pinhole for Linear. The engine reads the lens mode from metadata where it can (the GPMF spec defines field-of-view and projection tags; whether HERO13 photos write them is tested on a real file). - Close focus and resolution. The GoPro has fixed focus (minimum distance unpublished; measured on the camera); the Macro Lens Mod focuses at 11–75 cm and, like every lens mod, is calibrated as its own camera; a small Lego model fills little of the wide frame, so effective resolution on the object is low. Shoes and the book are better targets than Lego. - Capture. Time-lapse photo (a frame every 0.5 s) while walking around is the natural mode; video works but brings rolling shutter and motion blur. For video the frame-select stage picks sharp, well-spaced frames. - Metadata. GPMF carries gyro, accelerometer and gravity; gravity gives "up", and gyro could seed rotations (no evidence found yet of GPMF used as an SfM prior — an opening, not a given). - Into the system. SD card or USB to the laptop, then upload; Open GoPro over Wi-Fi later.

    Verdict. Works for medium objects in Linear mode; Wide mode needs the fisheye model to be good; small objects are a poor fit. Options: fisheye camera model; frame selection for video and time-lapse; GPMF gravity as "up".

    UC-A3 / UC-A4 · Object with the Nikon D850 and the Sony A1¶

    Differs from A1: - Files. NEF and ARW RAW, 45.7 MP and 50.1 MP, tens of MB each — 150 photos is several GB. LibRaw lists both cameras; whether the A1's compressed ARW variants decode correctly in rawpy is unverified (RC-P5). - Develop. This is the case the develop stage exists for: one white balance and one exposure across the set, a neutral tone curve, no per-photo look. Without it the texture shows seams. - Resolution pyramid. Matching at full 45–50 MP is slow and unnecessary; the engine extracts features at a reduced size and keeps full resolution for dense depth and texture. - Lens. A prime or a zoom fixed at one focal length; aperture f/5.6–f/8; a tripod or a fast shutter. The lens model from EXIF initialises calibration; a zoom moved mid-set splits the set into calibration groups, which the engine detects from EXIF focal length. - Turntable option. With a DSLR on a tripod and the object on a turntable, the background is static while the object turns. The background must be masked out or be featureless, or it pulls the solution towards "the camera never moved". This is the object-turntable recipe. - Scale. A printed AprilTag board beside the object gives metric scale and up.

    Verdict. The best object quality of the four devices; the heaviest data; the strongest test of the develop stage and of distribution (feature extraction over hundreds of large images fans out well). Options: RAW develop; resolution pyramid; calibration groups by focal length; turntable recipe with static-background exclusion; marker-based scale.

    4. Case B — a room¶

    UC-B1 · Room with the iPhone 15 Pro¶

    What makes a room different from an object: the camera is inside the subject looking out. Walls are often plain (little texture); windows and mirrors reflect; lighting is mixed (daylight and lamps); furniture occludes. And the classic mistake: standing in the middle and turning on the spot gives no parallax — pure rotation is the degenerate case, and nothing triangulates.

    • Capture. Walk along the walls, facing across the room, in overlapping passes at two heights; corners and doorways shot deliberately. Two modes:
    • Photos-only — the Camera app. Plain walls will resist registration.
    • Pose-and-depth — a capture app's export with ARKit poses and LiDAR depth. This is where the iPhone's extra sensors matter most: ARKit poses hold the solution together across plain walls, LiDAR depth gives geometry where photos have no texture, and the scale is metric.
    • Recipe. interior: sequential and retrieval pair selection, ARKit priors if present, the learned first pass (MapAnything) when texture is low, TSDF fusion from LiDAR depth alongside photo-based dense depth.
    • Registration. Photos-only: expect the room to break into pieces at plain walls; the engine reports the separate components and where the links are missing ("the wall between the window and the door has no overlap"). With priors: one model.
    • Scale and frame. Metric from ARKit, gravity-aligned; or one tape-measured wall length entered in the UI.
    • Products. A metric mesh for measuring and for robotics-lab comparisons; a splat for viewing, where a room looks far better than as a mesh.
    • Privacy. A room scan holds screens, documents and photos on walls; the project is marked "never leaves home" by default.

    Verdict. Photos-only is the honest stress test and may fail at plain walls; pose-and-depth is expected to work and is the case for the capture-app route. Options: pose priors; depth fusion; learned first pass for low texture; component reporting; the default "stays home" for interiors.

    UC-B2 · Room with the GoPro¶

    • The wide field of view helps a room: more overlap per frame, more texture in each image, fewer photos needed. The fisheye model is required.
    • Indoors the GoPro's small sensor raises noise and lengthens exposure; video blurs in low light. Time-lapse photo in good light, or video with plenty of light.
    • No depth, no poses; GPMF gravity gives "up". Scale arbitrary without a measured distance.

    Verdict. Likely registers better than iPhone photos-only thanks to the field of view; detail is lower. Options: fisheye model; frame selection; low-light warnings at ingest (ISO and shutter read from EXIF).

    UC-B3 / UC-B4 · Room with the Nikon D850 and the Sony A1¶

    • A wide lens (or the widest the owner has) and a tripod for low light; mixed light makes white balance across the set matter even more — the develop stage again.
    • Highest detail and the largest data; the case where distribution and rented workers start to matter.
    • No poses, no depth; scale from a tape measure or a marker on the wall.

    Verdict. Works where there is texture; plain walls resist as with the iPhone photos-only. Options: as UC-B1 photos-only, plus RAW develop and tripod-aware capture advice.

    5. Case C — a car in a car park¶

    • Size and height. About 4.5 m long and 1.5 m high; the roof needs views from above — a step ladder, a pole, or the DJI Mini 4 Pro (a natural moment to bring the drone in).
    • Surfaces. Glossy paint and glass reflect the surroundings, and reflections move with the viewpoint: false matches, holes in the mesh. Overcast light is far better than sun; a polarising filter on the DSLR cuts reflections.
    • Outdoors. Other cars, people, moving shadows: masks and a moving-object filter. Sun moves over a long capture; exposure locked anyway.
    • Recipe. object-walkaround at large scale, or aerial for the roof pass when the drone is used, merged into one model through shared images.
    • Scale. The wheelbase from the car's specification, or a tape-measured distance; GPS (phone or drone) gives rough scale and north.
    • Products. The splat is the showpiece; the mesh is good on matte parts (tyres, trim) and holed on glass. Paint reconstructs if overcast.

    Verdict. Works as a splat; works partially as a mesh; the drone pass is what makes the roof whole. Options: reflective-surface handling (masks, robust filtering); moving-object masks; multi-device merge (ground photos plus drone); scale from a known dimension.

    6. Case D — the motorbikes¶

    • Chrome, polished metal and black engine parts; thin cables and spokes; deep occlusions between engine, frame and wheels.
    • Capture in rings at several heights plus close passes into the engine bay; overcast light or a shaded garage.
    • The mesh struggles with thin and shiny parts; the splat handles them far better.
    • Two motorbikes: the same recipe twice — a first use of the benchmark idea across subjects.

    Verdict. Splat good, mesh partial; a strong showcase for "same poses, two products". Options: splat as the primary product for this subject type; mesh cleanup; close-pass capture advice.

    7. Case E — the mountain bike¶

    • The hardest object: thin tubes, spokes, cables, and very little surface per view — most of each photo is background seen through the bike.
    • Masks are mandatory; a plain background (a wall, a sheet) makes them reliable. On a work stand, the bike can be turned like a turntable — the object-turntable recipe with static background exclusion.
    • The mesh of the spokes is expected to fail; the frame and wheels' rims work. The splat renders the spokes acceptably (expected, not verified).

    Verdict. The stress test RC-P1's order puts last for a reason. Options: strong masking; turntable recipe; thin-structure handling is a later research item (mesh from splats).

    8. What the thought experiment shows¶

    What holds¶

    • The eight-step journey fits every case without a new component: ingest, recipe, registration, scale, products, export, quality and advice cover them all.
    • Recipes are necessary, not decoration. Within these first cases alone the engine needs at least object-walkaround, object-turntable, interior and aerial, each differing in pair selection, priors, masking and camera model.
    • Scale state earns its place. Half the cases end with arbitrary scale unless the owner adds a marker or a measurement; the engine must say so every time.
    • Same poses, two products pays off on exactly the owner's subjects: glass, chrome, spokes.

    What breaks, or needs care¶

    Problem Cases Answer in the design
    iPhone LiDAR and ARKit are not in Camera-app photos A1, B1 Two ingest routes: photos-only and pose-and-depth (capture-app export); the iPhone app later
    Per-photo processing in the iPhone (Smart HDR) A1, B1 ProRAW plus the develop stage; detect inconsistent tone across the set at ingest
    Automatic lens switching (macro) A1 Group by lens id from EXIF; calibrate per group; warn
    Safari HEIC to JPEG on upload A1, B1 Record what arrived; offer the Files route for originals
    Fisheye distortion A2, B2 Fisheye camera model in the recipe
    Rolling shutter and blur in video A2, B2 Frame selection; prefer time-lapse photos
    Repetitive texture (Lego studs) A1–A4 Geometric verification; masks; retrieval pairs
    Reflective and transparent surfaces A1 (camera), C, D Masks, robust filtering, splat as the faithful product
    Plain walls, pure rotation B1–B4 Pose priors, LiDAR depth, the learned first pass; capture advice ("move, don't turn")
    Static background on a turntable A3, A4, E object-turntable recipe with background exclusion
    Very large RAW sets A3, A4, B3, B4 Resolution pyramid; distribution; resumable upload
    Thin structures D, E Splat first; mesh-from-splat later
    Roof and top views C The drone; multi-device merge
    Moving people and cars outdoors C Moving-object masks

    Indoors against outdoors¶

    Concern Indoors Outdoors
    Light Low and mixed: noise, long exposures, white-balance mismatch Strong and moving: harsh shadows, specular highlights; overcast is best
    Background Static, often plain Busy and moving: people, cars, trees in wind
    Scale sources ARKit, LiDAR, tape, markers GPS (rough), known dimensions, markers, drone
    Typical recipe interior, object-turntable object-walkaround, aerial
    Privacy High (homes) — stays home by default Lower; rented workers allowed by default

    Engine capabilities this adds to the requirements¶

    1. Two ingest routes for the iPhone: photos-only and pose-and-depth (capture-app exports first).
    2. Calibration groups by lens and focal length from EXIF.
    3. A fisheye camera model alongside the pinhole and Brown models.
    4. Frame selection for video and time-lapse.
    5. A resolution pyramid: features at reduced size, dense and texture at full size.
    6. Automatic background masks, and moving-object masks outdoors.
    7. Recipes: object-walkaround, object-turntable (static background exclusion), interior (priors, depth fusion, learned first pass), aerial; multi-device merge across them.
    8. Scale entry: markers (AprilTag boards), one measured distance, or a known dimension.
    9. Ingest checks: tone inconsistency across the set, HEIC transcoding, low-light warnings.
    10. Component reporting when a capture breaks into pieces, with where the link is missing.
    11. Per-project "stays home" defaults by capture type.

    9. The owner's kit and answers (2026-11-23)¶

    • GoPro: HERO13 Black. (To verify: RAW photo (GPR) support and in which modes; lens modes available in photo and time-lapse; whether the HB-series lens mods — Macro, Ultra Wide — are worth testing for objects.)
    • Lenses: a wide range for both the D850 and the A1; the engine's recipes name what fits — a macro or short-tele prime around 90–105 mm at f/8–f/11 on the tripod for small objects (Lego, the camera), a normal prime around 35–50 mm for shoes, books and the motorbikes, a wide prime around 20–24 mm for the room and the car. One focal length per capture set.
    • Tripod: yes. Neutral background: yes. Turntable: improvised — the object-turntable recipe is tested from the start.
    • Markers and scale bars: acceptable in the first tests. A printed AprilTag board is the default scale and "up" source for object captures.
    • iPhone pose-and-depth route: the owner is ready to build an internal iPhone capture app now rather than depend on a third-party app. See RC-P11 in the brief.
